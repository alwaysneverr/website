I finished *The Sunlit Man* today.

It was really good, like an action movie that happens over a single day, in a Mad Max-inspired world, starring one of the characters from the mainline Brandon's Cosmere series that I didn't expect. Okay, I did expect it only because I already read the preview for this, but when it first released, I didn't really expect him to be the main character.

Anyway, pros of this story first. It's fast, pacing wise, but not breakneck speed that it gets to me. It's just enough to emulate the action movies that I liked to watch with my dad. The description of the fights are intense, and easily digestible. The plot itself actually gets to me a bit, nothing like a usually detached person finding himself caring for this one world as he gets stuck in it. Found family and stuff like that.

Cons of this story... there are parts of this story where I realized I'm out of my depth with the cosmere implications... kinda. I still can keep up with it, for sure, but they definitely did not hold back with showing the full breath of the Cosmere mechanics here, talking about stuff that I think only gets explored through many of the Words of Brandon and theorized in many circles instead of shown in the book... unless all of this happens in *The Lost Metal*, which I have *not* read yet. I do need to fast forward with the re-reading of *Hero of Ages*, cause it's been so long since I read *Era 2* that I already forgot the advancement that they have in that era.

And since this is my first read, I skipped over a ton of his environmental exposition, which again made me realize that he does that a lot. This part reminds me a lot of Stormlight, but in that book it's necessary to do that since they don't really have an art for the place they're currently at in the story... for this book though, they do have arts for important scenes, and the environment itself, so I feel like the explanation is a bit superfluous... but it also gives hint to the final twist, which I completely missed, so I guess it's forgivable.
