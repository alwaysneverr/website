I'm always a step behind.

As I see the outline of that person, illuminated by the avalanche of camera flashes, his smile beaming as he basked in the afterglow of his victory, I already began to reflect.

I'm always a step behind.

I was there when he first won his competition, winning second behind him. He won with his design called the Monarch. What I saw was familiar to that day, but he seems less shy now in comparison.

I'm always a step behind.

After that, either through a one-sided competition, or maybe just dumb luck, we kept meeting each other at various competitions regarding technological advancement. He managed to win every single time, with his designs growing more and more intriguing, and practical. I was forced to keep up in order to even get to his level, but as always...

I'm always a step behind.

What makes him different? What drives him to this extent, that I was unable to keep up with him? What differentiate us to this degree that I just cannot seem to win against him?

I'm always a step behind.

The flashes begin to recede as the celebration of the competition's end has come to its end. I saw my friends in the crowd and beamed and waved at them. Their appearance almost feels like something that has to happen, as they always cheered for me even when I didn't win. I peeped at him one last time, and notices that he already begun to leave. No one approached him, and he doesn't look at anyone else either. I knew he is already thinking of the next competition, the next design.

I'm always a step behind.

Perhaps that is our differences. He does not have anything else in his minds but his designs. He lives and breathes designs, finding new ways to innovate on current technology, and nothing else, not even friends.

Not even friends...

I'm always a step behind. But now I find myself stepping behind him. Tapping on his shoulder.

"Hey. Congratulations for yet another win."

I am pleased to see him flustered. No one ever approached him like this. I would know, I pay attention to his back this whole time.

"T-Thank you." He said, awkwardly. "I saw your design as well. A brand new way to experience dreams, through the use of looping. I think it was a close call this time around."

I looked at him, incredulous. "You're kidding me."

He looked at me confused. I know he think of nothing else but designs, but surely he would know.

"This is YOUR design, or at least part of it. The Monarch, rings a bell?"

His face turns cold as I mentioned the very project that inspired me to make my design for this competition. His works at the time was broad, encompassing multiple features, and by the end, he was only able to showcase a little of the list of features he has planned. I was sure that that time, he only won because of how his list of planned features that never came to be was more interesting than his prototype that he ended up making.

"You didn't even remember, did you?" I said, looking away, part embarassed(*sic*), part annoyed. This has always been a one-sided rivalry, and now I have proof of it. Before I can turn around and forget this ever happened, he put his hands on my shoulder.

I turned around to look at him, shocked. His face was now aghast, his victorious smile no longer in sight, as he said to me. "It worked?"
