What does one do in a funeral?

Like... really, what do you do as a person attending one? Is it just a symbol of respect to the dead that you are there in the first place? Are you allowed to laugh? Is it too much if you are on your phone all the way through? How long are you supposed to stay? Do you talk to the family of the deceased? Do you talk to other funeral goers?

How are you supposed to act?

All these question floated my mind as I stand inside the funeral home, my hand absentmindedly on his shoulder.

I never had to attend any family funeral before. I don't know what I should do.

I feel him shuddering from my palm,  and I hold him again, whilst the body of his mother laying in the coffin, for the public to see and mourn for.

He wasn't crying when his father dies, because he was too busy comforting his griefing mother. I know that cause I was there, and I saw how much his eyes are shaking, threatening to burst with tears, but he held on resolutely.

Now that there's no one left, his own grief swallowed him like a typhoon in an ocean, and he is like a lone ship that is about to capsize.

Perhaps this is what I should be doing in a funeral, comforting the griefing people who were left behind. Even though I know his mother through his stories, I have never interacted closely with her.

She joked a lot about how he should just ask me to marry him, but we both just adamantly refuses right to her face.

And now she will never see him in a suit, marrying the love of his life. I feel something stirring within me.

I guess I have successfully detached myself emotionally about this for so long, yet the thought that I might be the reason why she will never see his son get married... saddens me.
