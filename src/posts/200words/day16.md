For as long as I breathe, I loathed hospitals.

A lot of people attribute it to the clinical feel of the layout, the stark white walls, the smell of disinfectant.

I hate it mostly because of how dull it is. There's nothing creatively engaging about hospital. It's just rows of seatings, filled with worried faces and the endless stream of coughing or heaving or whatnot.

It's absolutely dreary.

"Feeling glum, are we?" My companion said as she appear from the corner, bearing gifts in her hands. "They have these croissant in the vending machine. Thought we could share."

"That'd be splendid. Thanks." I took the bread from her hands and busied myself with handling it's opening.

"Better than nothing."

"Yes."

My partner stopped eating midway. "Pardon?"

"Yes, I'm feeling gloomy right now." I confessed, my eyes pointedly looking at anything but her eyes. 

I was told to always look into someone's eyes when I talk to them, but I like to default to avoiding them in times like this. In times when I don't like to talk about the topic.

"You have this bad habit..."

"I know."

"No, I don't think you do."

"Yeah, alright, here." I looked up and meet her inquisitive gaze. "It's the hospital."

"What... wait... did you think your bad habit is not looking at someone  else's eyes when you're talking to them?"

"It's... not?" I scratched my head a little.

"No. I meant you trying to say something... and then you just wait until the other person said something."

"O-oh."

"Just come out with it, bro. Don't be so dramatic." She said, continuing to eat her bread, eating it ravenously. She must've been very hungry.

"It's not like I do it consciously, you know." I ended up watching her eat her bread before realizing I haven't touched mine, and hurriedly do so. "Besides, people tend to interrupt me a lot, including y-"

"Me?"

I sighed. "Yes, exhibit A."

She rolled her eyes. "Okay, so, the hospital."

"The hospital. Yes." I sat up straight. "When I was little... there was this accident, where my parents got rear ended by a truck."

I started opening up to her about my experience. "I was not with them in the car, as they were celebrating their anniversary at the time, so I have this babysitter with me."

"I still remember her face when she got that call. I will never forget her dread-filled face throughout the car ride to the hospital."

"Once we arrived, though, the doctor had a talk with the sitter. I never really know what it was about, since a nurse took me by the hand and brought me to this playroom."

I shuddered a little bit. "And that's where the nightmare begins."

"The nightmare?"

"Yeah. What happened in that playroom made me loathe going to the hospital ever since. It's not like it's the only incident, but it starts there."

"Okay... what happened?" She said, her curious face apparent.

"Well... it's the worst thing that can ever happen to a mind as creative as mine..." I said, as I add a dramatic pause. It lasted long enough that I can see her eye roll, and I chuckled. "Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"Absolutely nothing happened."

"The fuck you mean, nothing happened?"

"There's literally ZERO things that I can play with in that playroom!" I said with what I think is an aghast face. "No toys, no paper I can draw, not even posters I can look at. The room is so squeaky clean, I thought I was being experimented on!"

"You thought of that when you were how old, again?"

"Seven years old. Doesn't matter, but like, LOOK at this place." I gestured to the place we're waiting right now. "It's just rows and rows of chairs."

I gestured to some people in the distance, sitting with their hands on their head. "Look at the sorrow." I whispered to her. "This place sucks."

"Dude."

"What?"

"I don't know if this is... some kind of coping mechanism or whatever, but..." She rushed in for a hug.

"I'm here for you, okay?"

"I... I'm just saying, it's just very dreary..."

"Yeah, yeah. Okay, buddy." She patted my head. "It's okay."
She can see through it. The facade. "I guess that doesn't make sense, does it?"

"It really does not."

"I'm sorry."

"No, it's okay. We all have our quirks in dealing with these type of things."

I didn't let go of her yet, so her voice, as soft as a breeze, tickled my ears gently. It feels kinda funny, but also... endearing.

"Yeah... I... I just don't know what I should do right now."

She continued patting me in the head as she listened to my rant. "I mean, she did survive that accident, you know? So does my dad. No one got hurt."

"I see. So there was an accident."

"Yeah. But this time..."

This time... my dad's not here anymore.

"I... I hope she's okay."
